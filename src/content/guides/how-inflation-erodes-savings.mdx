## Key takeaway

Inflation is the quiet tax on cash: it lowers what your dollars can buy over time. A dollar saved today is worth less in ten years, so keeping too much in idle cash quietly loses purchasing power. The fix is to earn a return that at least keeps pace.

## Inflation is a rising price level, not a falling balance

Your bank balance does not shrink—the things you buy get more expensive. If inflation runs 3% a year, something that costs $100 today costs about $134 in ten years. Your $100 still says $100, but it buys less. That gap is the real cost of inflation, and it is invisible on a statement.

A simple rule of thumb: at 3% inflation, prices roughly double every 24 years. At 7%, every 10 years. The higher the rate, the faster your cash loses ground.

## Why idle cash loses

Cash in a non-interest account loses purchasing power every year by the full inflation rate. Cash in a savings account loses by the gap between inflation and your interest rate. If inflation is 3% and you earn 0.5%, you are losing about 2.5% of real value annually—slowly, steadily, and without drama.

The [Inflation Calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator/) shows what a sum will be worth in future dollars, and the gap between that and your actual return is your true loss.

## A worked example

Say you set aside $20,000 for a goal ten years out and leave it in a 0.5% account while inflation averages 3%. The balance grows to about $21,028—but in today's purchasing power that is worth only about $15,600. You "gained" $1,028 and lost roughly $4,400 of buying power. The [Compound Interest Calculator](/calculators/compound-interest-calculator/) makes the same point in the other direction: a return above inflation preserves and builds real wealth.

## The mistake everyone makes: confusing nominal and real

People celebrate a balance that went up, forgetting it bought less. The number on the screen is nominal; what matters is real—adjusted for prices.

Other errors:

- **Keeping the whole emergency fund invested:** some cash must stay safe and liquid; inflation protection applies to money you will not need soon.
- **Assuming "safe" means "no loss":** idle cash is safe from market drops but not from inflation.
- **Ignoring taxes on top:** a 3% return taxed at 25% is 2.25% net, which may still trail inflation.
- **Chasing yield recklessly:** the answer is a sensible return, not a risky one.

## How to stay ahead

1. **Know your real return:** return minus inflation minus tax.
2. **Keep only what you need liquid** in cash; move longer-horizon money to investments that historically outpace inflation.
3. **Use the [Inflation Calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator/)** to see the erosion before you park a large sum.
4. **Revisit yearly:** a rising inflation rate changes the math fast.
5. **Match the tool to the timeline:** near-term cash stays safe; long-term cash should grow.
