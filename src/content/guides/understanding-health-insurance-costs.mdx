## Key takeaway

Health insurance has four numbers that decide your real cost: premium, deductible, copay, and out-of-pocket max. The cheapest premium is often the most expensive plan if you actually use care. Pick the plan by your expected usage, not the monthly price.

## The four numbers that matter

- **Premium:** what you pay every month just to have the plan, whether you see a doctor or not.
- **Deductible:** what you pay out of pocket before insurance starts sharing costs.
- **Copay or coinsurance:** your share of each visit or service after the deductible is met.
- **Out-of-pocket maximum:** the most you pay in a year; after that, the plan pays 100%.

A plan with a low premium usually has a high deductible, and vice versa. The [Health Insurance Calculator](/calculators/health-insurance-calculator/) totals your cost across these for a typical year of usage.

## How usage flips the answer

A worked example for two plans:

- **Plan A:** $300/month premium, $6,000 deductible, low coinsurance.
- **Plan B:** $520/month premium, $1,000 deductible, moderate coinsurance.

If you rarely see a doctor, Plan A costs about $3,600 a year in premiums and little else. Plan B costs $6,240 before any care. Plan A wins.

But if you have a $10,000 medical year: Plan A is $3,600 + $6,000 deductible + some coinsurance ≈ $10,000+. Plan B is $6,240 + $1,000 + coinsurance, capped near its out-of-pocket max—often less. For a heavy-usage year, the "expensive" plan is cheaper. The calculator makes this break-even visible.

## The mistake everyone makes: choosing by premium alone

People pick the lowest monthly payment and are shocked by the deductible when care arrives. The premium is the only number you see every month; the deductible is the number that hurts once.

Other errors:

- **Ignoring the network:** out-of-network care can be uncovered entirely.
- **Forgetting the out-of-pocket max:** it is your true worst case and should anchor the decision.
- **Overlooking an HSA:** high-deductible plans often pair with a tax-advantaged HSA that softens the deductible.
- **Not counting prescriptions:** a cheap plan that excludes your medication is not cheap.

## How to choose

1. **Estimate your yearly usage**—few visits, a couple, or a chronic condition.
2. **Run both plans** through the [Health Insurance Calculator](/calculators/health-insurance-calculator/) at that usage.
3. **Anchor on the worst case:** compare out-of-pocket maximums plus premiums.
4. **Check the network and drug list** before anything else.
5. **Use an HSA if offered:** it lowers the effective cost of a high-deductible plan.
