## Key takeaway

ROI tells you the total gain or loss on an investment as a percentage; CAGR tells you the smooth yearly rate that produced it. ROI hides the time it took; CAGR reveals it. Use both and you stop comparing a one-year win to a ten-year win.

## ROI is the score; CAGR is the pace

Return on Investment (ROI) is the simplest measure:

> **ROI = (gain − cost) ÷ cost × 100**

Put in $1,000, get out $1,300, and your ROI is 30%. Clean, but silent on time. A 30% ROI in one month is spectacular; in ten years it is mediocre. That silence is ROI's flaw.

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) fixes it by spreading the return evenly across the years:

> **CAGR = (ending value ÷ starting value)^(1 / years) − 1**

It answers: "what steady yearly rate would get me from start to finish?" That makes different investments comparable.

## A worked example

You invest $10,000 and it grows to $16,105 over five years.

- ROI = ($16,105 − $10,000) ÷ $10,000 = **61%** total.
- CAGR = ($16,105 ÷ $10,000)^(1/5) − 1 ≈ **10% per year**.

Now compare: Investment A returns 61% over 5 years (10% CAGR). Investment B returns 30% over 1 year (30% CAGR). ROI alone makes A look better (61% > 30%), but B grew far faster per year. CAGR tells the truth the ROI hides.

## The mistake everyone makes: comparing ROIs across different time spans

A common error is ranking investments by ROI without checking the years. A 50% ROI over a decade is about 4.1% a year—barely ahead of inflation. A 20% ROI over one year is 20% a year. Side by side, the "smaller" ROI wins.

Other errors:

- **Ignoring costs and taxes:** ROI should use what you actually keep, not the headline gain.
- **Treating CAGR as a guarantee:** it smooths bumps; real years were up and down.
- **Forgetting the start point:** CAGR is sensitive to a particularly high or low starting value.
- **Mixing it with yield:** dividends are part of return but are not the same as price growth.

## How to read returns

1. **Compute ROI** for the total gain on a deal—use the [ROI Calculator](/calculators/roi-calculator/).
2. **Annualize with CAGR** before comparing to anything held a different length—use the [CAGR Calculator](/calculators/cagr-calculator/).
3. **Subtract costs and taxes** so the comparison is real, not paper.
4. **Compare like periods:** only line up CAGRs, never raw ROIs.
5. **Check the risk:** a high CAGR from a wild ride is not the same as a steady one.
